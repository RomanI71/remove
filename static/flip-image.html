When to Flip vs. Mirror an Image

Understanding how to use orientation can change the impact of your visual content. Here are the most common ways our users utilize this tool:

  • Correcting Selfies: Most front-facing cameras show you a "mirror view" while you take the photo but save the "natural view." Flipping horizontally restores the look you saw on your screen.
  • Creative Symmetry: Duplicate an image and flip one side to create a beautiful, perfectly symmetrical "kaleidoscope" effect for social media backgrounds.
  • Fixing Text Orientation: If you've captured an image through a mirror and the text is backwards, a horizontal flip will make the text readable again.
  • Artistic Perspective: Sometimes, flipping an image vertically (upside down) can create a unique "reflection" look, especially useful for water or landscape photography.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Is there a difference between "Rotating" and "Flipping"?

A: Yes! Rotating turns the image around a center point (like turning a wheel), while flipping creates a mirror reflection across an axis. Flipping is used to change the "facing" direction of the subject.

Q: Can I flip an image on my iPhone or Android?

A: Absolutely. Our website is fully mobile-responsive. You can upload a photo directly from your camera roll, flip it, and save it back to your phone instantly.

Q: Does this tool work with animated GIFs?

A: This specific tool is optimized for static images (JPG, PNG, WebP). For animated GIFs, only the first frame will be flipped and saved as a static image.
